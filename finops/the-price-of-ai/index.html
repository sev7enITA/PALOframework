Imagine the platform team halves its AI bill, where accounts payable inherits the review queue and both teams can report their numbers accurately while the company pays more. Matt Bellio’s IBM Think Club article follows an AI coding task through repeated context, retries and developer review. I’d put one question to the people approving the rollout: 𝘄𝗵𝗼 𝗴𝗲𝘁𝘀 𝘁𝗼 𝗰𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗺 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘀𝗮𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴, 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝘄𝗵𝗼 𝗵𝗮𝘀 𝘁𝗼 𝗳𝗶𝗻𝗶𝘀𝗵 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸?

Take an illustrative invoice-exception workflow: each route attempts the same 1,000 cases under common acceptance criteria. Route A spends €120 on the provider and €140 on operations and review. It delivers 950 accepted cases at €0.274 each. Route B halves provider spend to €60. Operations and review rise to €240, and it delivers 900 at €0.333 each. Provider spend falls 50% while the cost per accepted case rises 22%. To compare either route with the existing process, price the work still needed to resolve the other 50 or 100 cases.

▪️ 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗲𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 traces repeated context, cache misses, tool calls, retries and fallback. Switching routes can mean replaying context and repeating verification. A timeout can leave two questions open. Did the provider charge us? Did the payment actually go through? Resolve the second before retrying a payment instruction and reconcile the first before closing the books.

▪️ The 𝗯𝘂𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀 process owner defines what counts as resolved, measures review effort and tracks reopened cases. A duplicate payment discovered after acceptance adds recovery work that the original KPI can miss. Segment by case difficulty. Average handling time can fall while the hardest cases pile up for the same two senior reviewers.

▪️ The 𝗲𝘅𝗲𝗰𝘂𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 sponsor owns the decision to fund, scale or stop. Finance reconciles shared costs and asks whether the hours released translated into lower spending or capacity the business used. Procurement prices the exit, including parallel operation and requalification. Risk and assurance need a formal hold when material errors breach agreed limits. A supplier disputing a payment may spot the failure before the dashboard does.

In the 𝗣𝗔𝗟𝗢 𝗔𝗜 𝗙𝗶𝗻𝗢𝗽𝘀 𝗺𝗼𝗱𝘂𝗹𝗲, reserve budget before an attempt and keep it committed while the charge is unknown. A reservation governs local budget admission. The provider may still charge more. Reconcile the costs of successful and failed attempts in the same cohort, and count each accepted case once. Withhold the final ratio while billing or outcome evidence is incomplete. At the next AI budget review, I’d ask the process owner to bring the failed and reopened cases alongside the invoice. Show who finished them, how much time it took and whose budget paid for it.
